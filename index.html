Plate I · The whole family

The Order of Arda

A who's who of Tolkien's world on one page: from Eru, the One, down through the Holy Ones to Elves, Men, Dwarves, Ents and the servants of the Dark. Box colours show how each kind came to be. Gold numbers send you to the matching Notes on pages 8 to 10.

Eru Ilúvatar

the One · All-Father

Stands outside the World he imagined. Every living thing traces back to him, and he alone can give a creature a true life of its own.1

The Children of Ilúvatar

Elves and Men

Eru's own idea, added to the Music without the Ainur's help. They woke in Middle-earth at the time he chose.6, 7

Elves

Quendi · the Firstborn

Woke by the lake of Cuiviénen under starlight. They live as long as the World does; if slain, they go to the Halls of Mandos.

Eldar and Avari

see Plate III

Those who took up the Valar's call to go West, and those who stayed behind. The travellers split into Vanyar, Noldor and Teleri.

The Half-elven

Elrond, Elros, Arwen

Born of marriages between Elves and Men, and given a choice of which kin to belong to. See Plate V.13

Men

Atani · the Secondborn

Woke at the first sunrise, far in the East. Mortal: when they die they leave the World altogether, which the Elves called the Gift of Men.

Númenóreans

the Dúnedain

Elf-friends given an island kingdom. When it sank, survivors founded Arnor and Gondor; Aragorn is their heir.12

Hobbits

Halflings

A small, quiet branch of Men, later settled in the Shire.15

Other peoples

see Plate IV

Rohirrim, Bardings, Beornings, Easterlings, Haradrim, Dunlendings and the Woses.

The Ainur

the Holy Ones

Spirits Eru thought into being before time began. Their Great Music became the pattern of the World, Eä. Some chose to go down into it.1

Maiar

The helpers and servants of the Valar. Many of them, and few named.4

Istari

Melian

Queen of Doriath

Arien and Tilion

guides of Sun and Moon

Each steers one of the two lights across the sky.5

Valar

The mightiest Ainur in the World, who shaped it and guard it from Valinor in the far West. Manwë is their king.2

Dwarves

Ents

Spirits who came to dwell in trees as guardians of the woods.9

Great Eagles

Wise, speaking birds who carry news and rescue.9

Melkor · Morgoth

Once the greatest of the Ainur. He broke the Music with his own themes and spoiled the World. He can twist life but never make it.3, 10

Fallen spirits

Began among Aulë's people; became Morgoth's chief servant, then a Dark Lord himself.4

Balrogs

fallen Maiar

Fire-spirits who joined Melkor early.

Bred or twisted

creatures of the Dark

Orcs

Most tales say bred from captured Elves.10

Trolls

Huge and slow; daylight turns many to stone.10

Dragons

Glaurung, Ancalagon, Smaug.10

Werewolves

Evil spirits in wolf shape.

Ungoliant → Shelob

origin unknown

A spider-shaped being of darkness who helped Melkor kill the Two Trees. Shelob was the last of her brood.16

Two awakenings

The Elves woke first, in a world lit only by stars. Men woke much later, on the morning the Sun first rose. That is why Elves are called the Firstborn and Men the Secondborn.6, 7

How to read the lines

A line downwards means "came from" or "belongs to". A dashed red line means rebellion. Double gold lines on Plates II and V join husbands and wives. A box's colour tells you how that kind of being came into the World (see the key below).

The rule behind it all

Real life is Eru's alone to give. Melkor and Sauron could breed, enslave or twist living things, but never make anything new.10

How the World and the Ainur came to be1

1 · The thought

Before anything else existed, Eru thought the Ainur into being and gave each a part of the song.

2 · The Music

Together they sang a great Music on themes Eru gave them. Melkor wove in his own loud themes and caused discord.

3 · The vision

Eru showed them their Music as a picture of a world. Many of them longed to see it made real.

4 · Eä

Eru said "Let it be" (Eä in Elvish), and the World existed, though still unshaped and waiting.

5 · The descent

Any Ainu could enter, but had to stay until the World's end. The mighty became Valar; the rest, Maiar.

Plate III · The Firstborn

The Elves and the Great Parting

The Elves first opened their eyes by Cuiviénen, a lake lit only by stars. From then on they kept dividing over one question: whether to make the long journey West to the Valar.6

Eru Ilúvatar

Elves were Eru's own idea; the Valar had no part in it.

The Quendi

"those who speak with voices"

The first Elves, awake by Cuiviénen in the far East while only starlight shone. A later story says there were 144 at first, in three companies, under Imin, Tata and Enel.6

Taken by Melkor

Before Oromë found them, Elves who wandered off were snatched by Melkor's creatures. Most tales say Orcs were bred from these captives.10

The Eldar

"people of the stars"

Oromë found them and the Valar invited them to Valinor. Those who said yes set out on the Great Journey.

Avari

"the unwilling"

Refused the journey and stayed in the East; their story is mostly untold.

Vanyar

Fair Elves

Their king Ingwë was High King of all Elves. Every one of them reached Valinor, and few ever came back.

Who's who

Ingwë, their king.

Indis, Finwë's second wife; Fingolfin and Finarfin were her sons.

Noldor

Deep Elves

Led by Finwë. Lovers of knowledge and craft, friends of Aulë. Fëanor made the Silmarils.11

Noldor in Exile

After Morgoth stole the Silmarils, most of the Noldor followed Fëanor back to Middle-earth to make war on him.11

Who's who

Fëanor, who made the Silmarils.

Fingolfin and Finarfin, his half-brothers.

Galadriel, Finarfin's daughter.

Finrod, her brother, friend of Men.

Turgon, king of Gondolin.

Gil-galad, last High King in Middle-earth.16

Celebrimbor, Fëanor's grandson, maker of the Three Rings.14

Teleri

the Last-comers

The biggest company and the slowest, always lingering. Two brothers, Elwë and Olwë, were their kings. Along the way they split three ways.

Falmari

Sea-elves

Under Olwë they finally crossed the sea and built Alqualondë, the Swan Haven, on the shores of Valinor.

Who's who

Olwë, king at Alqualondë.

Eärwen, his daughter; Galadriel's mother.

Sindar

Grey-elves

Stayed in Beleriand searching for Elwë, who was lost in a wood after meeting Melian. As King Thingol he later ruled them.

Who's who

Thingol and Melian, king and queen of Doriath.

Lúthien, their daughter (Plate V).

Círdan, the Shipwright.

Celeborn, Thingol's kinsman, Galadriel's husband.

Thranduil, king in Mirkwood; father of Legolas.

Nandor

those who turned back

Led by Lenwë, they stopped at the Misty Mountains. Their descendants include the Silvan Elves of the woods and the Green-elves.

Who's who

Silvan Elves of Lothlórien and Mirkwood.

Amroth, a lord of Lórien in the Third Age.

The Half-elven

Peredhil

Elrond and his children come from Sindar, Noldor and Men all at once. They were given a choice of fate. See Plate V.13

Three who went ahead

Oromë first took Ingwë, Finwë and Elwë to see Valinor for themselves. They came back amazed by the light of the Trees, and talked many of their people into the journey.

An island for a ship

Ulmo ferried the Vanyar and Noldor over the sea on a floating island. The Teleri came later; Ossë taught them about ships, and swans finally drew their fleet to Valinor.

The hidden kingdom

Thingol and Melian ruled the forest realm of Doriath. Melian's power wrapped it in an unseen fence that no enemy could get through, until treachery from within.

By the Third Age

The Elves who remained lived mostly in Rivendell, Lothlórien, Mirkwood and the Grey Havens. One by one they took ship into the West.

Calaquendi, Elves of the Light: those who lived in Valinor and saw the Two Trees.
Moriquendi, Elves of the Darkness: those who never saw the Trees' light. Úmanyar means those who set out but never reached Valinor.
Plate IV · The Secondborn

Men, Hobbits and the Kingdoms of Men

The Secondborn arrived with the first sunrise. Some befriended the Elves and grew great, some never met them, and some fell under the Shadow. Hobbits belong to this family too.7

Eru Ilúvatar

Planned Men himself, in a later theme of the Music.7

Men

the Secondborn · Atani · the Followers

Woke in Hildórien, far in the East, as the Sun first rose. Restless and short-lived. At death they leave the World for somewhere not even the Valar know: the Gift of Men.7

Three kinds of Men

Faramir of Gondor once sorted Men three ways: the High (from Númenor), the Middle (Rohan, for example) and the Wild.

Edain

Elf-friends · three houses

Three houses, Bëor's, Haleth's and Hador's, crossed into Beleriand and fought beside the Elves against Morgoth.

Númenóreans

the Dúnedain · Men of the West

Rewarded with Númenor, an island far out in the sea, and three times a normal lifespan. One rule: never sail west out of sight of it.12

The Faithful

Elendili

Kept faith with the Valar and the Elves; their leaders were the Lords of Andúnië.

Elendil

with his sons Isildur and Anárion

Fled the sinking in nine ships and set up two kingdoms in exile.

Arnor

The North. Isildur's line.

Gondor

The South. Kings, then Stewards.

Dúnedain → Aragorn

Arnor's heirs became the Rangers of the North. Aragorn, their chieftain, was crowned King Elessar.13

The King's Men

Grew jealous of the Elves' long life and came to hate the Ban. Their last king, Ar-Pharazôn, brought Sauron back as a captive, and he became their adviser and ruin.12

Black Númenóreans

Númenórean colonists who went over to Sauron, such as his Mouth and some of the Nazgûl.

Why Númenor fell

Wanting the Valar's undying lands, the last king sailed to attack them. Eru changed the shape of the World, and Númenor sank under the sea.12

Middle Peoples

Men of the Twilight

Relatives of the Edain who stayed behind, far east of Beleriand.

Rohirrim

Riders of Rohan

Horse-lords from the north who rode to save Gondor and were given the land of Rohan. Théoden, Éomer and Éowyn are theirs.

Dale and Lake-town

Bard the Bowman shot Smaug and became King of Dale.

Beornings

Beorn could take the shape of a great bear.

Woodmen

Hardy folk living on the western edge of Mirkwood.

Men of Darkness

the Wild

Those who never journeyed west to meet the Elves. Many were deceived by Morgoth and then by Sauron, but not all.

Easterlings

Peoples of the East; many fought for Sauron.

Haradrim

Southrons

From the hot South; they brought great war-beasts.

Dunlendings

Hill-folk with an old grudge against Rohan; Saruman stirred them up.

Drúedain

Woses · Wild Men

An ancient woodland people, no friends of Sauron; Ghân-buri-Ghân guided Rohan's riders.

Nazgûl

Ringwraiths

Nine great Men bound to Sauron by his Rings.14

Hobbits

Halflings · Periannath

A small, cheerful branch of Men. Where they began is not known; the earliest stories place them in the Vales of Anduin.15

Three breeds

Harfoots, small and the most usual; Stoors, stockier and fond of rivers; Fallohides, taller, fairer and friends of Elves.

The Shire

Settled in the year 1601 of the Third Age by the brothers Marcho and Blanco.

Bilbo, Frodo, Sam, Merry, Pippin

Bilbo found the One Ring; Frodo and Sam carried it to Mount Doom; Merry and Pippin joined the Fellowship.

Sméagol · Gollum

Of a river folk close to the Stoors. Nearly five centuries with the Ring wore him away.

The kingdoms of Men at a glance

First Age

The three houses of the Edain fight beside the Elves in Beleriand. Beren, Túrin and Tuor come from them.

Second Age

Númenor rises, grows proud, and sinks (year 3319). Elendil's exiles found Arnor and Gondor.

Third Age

Arnor breaks up and fades; its heirs become Rangers. Gondor's kings fail and Stewards rule. Rohan is founded (year 2510).

War of the Ring

Third Age 3018 to 3019. Gondor and Rohan stand together while Frodo and Sam destroy the One Ring.

Fourth Age

Aragorn, crowned King Elessar, rules Arnor and Gondor together again; the age of Men begins.

The Choice of the Half-elven

Eärendil and Elwing reached Valinor to plead for Elves and Men. The Valar let them and their sons choose a kin. Elrond took the Elves' fate, Elros took Men's. Elrond's children could choose too; Arwen gave up Elvish life to marry Aragorn.13

Three marriages of Elves and Men

Beren and Lúthien, Tuor and Idril, and Aragorn and Arwen. Each tied the two peoples closer and changed the history of the World.

How are Aragorn and Arwen related?

Very distantly. Both come from Eärendil and Elwing: Arwen through Elrond, Aragorn through Elros and many generations of kings. Galadriel is Arwen's grandmother.

Four bloodlines meet in Elrond

Thingol brings Sindarin blood, Melian a Maia's, Turgon the Noldor's, and Beren and Tuor the blood of Men.

Reading this tree

Down lines run from parents to children. Double gold lines join husband and wife. The dashed gold line is the marriage of Aragorn and Arwen, many generations apart.

Plate VI · Made by the Valar

Dwarves, Ents and Eagles

Three peoples that began with a Vala's wish, and the moment each became truly alive. Only Eru can give that, so each story has a moment where he steps in.8, 9

Aulë the Smith

Tired of waiting for the Children of Ilúvatar, he longed for people to teach his crafts to.8

1 · Made in secret

In a hall under the mountains he shaped the Seven Fathers of the Dwarves and invented a language for them, Khuzdul.

2 · Eru's question

Eru asked why he had tried this. His Dwarves could only move when Aulë thought about them, like puppets.

3 · The hammer

Ashamed, Aulë lifted his hammer to break them. They flinched and begged for mercy, alive in their own right.

4 · Adopted

Eru had given them life. But they had to sleep in stone until after the Elves had woken.

The Dwarves · Khazâd

the Seven Fathers and their peoples

Tough, proud and stubborn, masters of stone and metal, and slow to forget a wrong. They believe Aulë gathers their spirits after death.8

Longbeards

Durin's Folk

The senior house. Their father Durin woke under Mount Gundabad and later built Khazad-dûm (Moria).

Firebeards, Broadbeams

Lived in Nogrod and Belegost in the Blue Mountains during the First Age.

Four eastern houses

Ironfists, Stiffbeards, Blacklocks and Stonefoots. Their stories are hardly told.

Who's who

Thrór, Thráin and Thorin Oakenshield: Durin's heirs; Thorin's quest is the story of The Hobbit.

Balin tried to win back Moria. Dáin Ironfoot became King under the Mountain. Gimli, Glóin's son, joined the Fellowship.

Yavanna Kementári

Queen of the Earth. She learned of the Dwarves and worried for her trees, which can't run away.9

1 · A worry

Dwarves would cut her forests without care. Who would speak up for the trees?

2 · A request

She asked Manwë. Thinking on it, Manwë saw again the Music and Eru's answer.

3 · The answer

When the Children woke, spirits would come to live in trees and guard them, and Eagles would live in the mountains, watching over the land.

The Ents · Onodrim

Shepherds of the Trees

Tall, slow and careful, part tree and part person. The Elves woke them to speech. They look after the forests as shepherds look after sheep.9

Treebeard

Fangorn

Eldest of the Ents still walking in the Third Age; Gandalf counted him the oldest living thing under the Sun.

Entwives

Preferred tidy gardens and fields to wild woods. They wandered away and were never found.

Huorns

nature uncertain

Trees that grew watchful and fierce, or Ents gone half-tree. Orcs who fled into them after Helm's Deep never came out.16

The Two Trees

Yavanna sang Telperion (silver) and Laurelin (gold) to light Valinor. When they were killed, their last flower and fruit became the Moon and the Sun.5

Manwë Súlimo

Ruler of Arda and master of the winds. He watches the World from his throne on Taniquetil.

The Great Eagles

Manwë's messengers

Huge, speaking birds who bring Manwë news and step in at desperate moments. Whether they were spirits in eagle shape or true living birds, Tolkien never quite settled.9, 16

Thorondor

First Age

King of Eagles. He scarred Morgoth's face, and carried away Fingolfin's body.

Gwaihir, Landroval

Third Age

Rescued Gandalf from Orthanc, and Frodo and Sam from Mount Doom.

Oromë the Huntsman

Hunter, rider and keeper of hounds, at home in the wild forests of Middle-earth.

Huan

Hound of Valinor

Given to the Elf-lord Celegorm. He helped Beren and Lúthien, and fell fighting the great wolf Carcharoth.

The Mearas

Shadowfax's line

Horses of Rohan's kings. Rohan's tale says Oromë brought their first sire from the West.

The rule behind it all

Life is Eru's alone to give. A Vala may build a body, or call a spirit to it, but cannot make it truly alive. That is why the Dark could only copy and corrupt, and never create.10

Plate VII · The Shadow

The Dark and Its Servants

Melkor had no power to create, so his followers were spirits he lured away, creatures he bred from living things, or peoples he tricked. Sauron did the same after him.10

Origins unknown

The many-armed thing in the pool by Moria's west gate, and the unnamed creatures Gandalf found gnawing the roots of the world far below Moria.16

Melkor · Morgoth

the great Enemy

Wrecked the Lamps, destroyed the Trees with Ungoliant's help and seized the Silmarils. Defeated at last in the War of Wrath and cast out of the World.3

Ungoliant

origin unknown · her own master

A spirit in spider form. She helped Melkor kill the Trees, then turned on him. The spiders of Mirkwood and Shelob came from her.16

Sauron

Strongholds of the Dark

Utumno

Melkor's first fortress, far in the North; broken by the Valar to protect the newly woken Elves.

Angband

The hill-fort in southern Mirkwood where Sauron hid as "the Necromancer".

Isengard

Gondor's old ring-wall with the tower Orthanc, where Saruman built his army.

Three ways the Dark gained servants

Spirits won over

Maiar who followed Melkor's splendour: Sauron and the Balrogs.

Bred from the living

Orcs, Trolls, dragons and wolves: copies or twisted versions of real creatures, never new life.

Peoples deceived

Men who served out of fear, lies or Ring-lust: the Nazgûl, Númenórean renegades, and many from the East and South.

Two Dark Lords

Morgoth meant to possess the whole World or wreck it, and spread his strength thin trying. Sauron wanted to be its master, and locked much of his strength inside the One Ring.3, 14

Notes: How It All Began

Each number matches a gold figure on the plates. Everything here is retold in our own words.

1Eru and the Music

Before there was anything, there was Eru, called Ilúvatar by the Elves, meaning All-Father. He is the single Creator in Tolkien's world. From his thought he made the Ainur, the Holy Ones, and gave each of them a part of his mind to understand.

He set them a great theme and they sang it together: the Music of the Ainur. Melkor, the mightiest of them, wanted to add things of his own, and his loud themes clashed with the rest. Eru answered with new themes, and twice more Melkor's noise rose against them. Finally Eru ended the Music with one great chord.

Then he showed the Ainur a vision of what they had sung: a world, and the story it would hold. Some longed to see it real. So Eru said "Eä!", "Let it be!", and the World existed. Ainur who loved it could go into it, but only by binding themselves to it until the end. The Elves and Men were Eru's own theme, which the Ainur had no part in, so they did not fully understand them.

2The Valar

Fourteen great Ainur entered the World and set about shaping it: seven lords and seven queens, the Valar or "Powers". Men in later days sometimes took them for gods, but Tolkien is clear they are created spirits, far greater than angels, who answer to Eru.

They can wear bodies like clothing, and most often appear as tall, noble lords and ladies. Manwë is their king and Varda their queen. Eight of them, the Aratar, are the greatest: Manwë and Varda, Ulmo, Aulë and Yavanna, Oromë, Mandos and Nienna.

After Melkor ruined their first home in the middle of the World, they withdrew to Valinor, in Aman, the land far in the West, and walled it with mountains.

3Melkor becomes Morgoth

Melkor, "he who arises in might", began with more power and knowledge than any other Ainu. He went often into the empty Void looking for the Imperishable Flame, the power to make life, which belongs to Eru alone. He never found it.

In the World he fought the Valar from the beginning, spoiling whatever they made. He threw down the two great Lamps that lit the World, built fortresses in the North, and later, with Ungoliant, killed the Two Trees of Valinor and stole the Silmarils. The Elf Fëanor then gave him the name Morgoth, the Dark Enemy of the World, and it stuck.

He poured so much of himself into ruling and twisting the World that his power grew thin. When the First Age closed, the War of Wrath broke his power, and the Valar shut him out of the World, in the Void.

4The Maiar and the Wizards

The Maiar are spirits like the Valar, only less mighty. Nobody knows how many there are; few are named. Some served a particular Vala: Eönwë and Manwë, Ossë and Ulmo, Arien and Vána.

Melkor won some of them over in his early days: the Balrogs, and the greatest of his servants, Sauron, who had once been a Maia of Aulë.

Around Third Age 1000 the Valar sent five Maiar to Middle-earth to help its peoples stand against Sauron. They came looking like old men, were not allowed to fight his might with might, and were called the Istari, or Wizards. Saruman (Curumo) came from Aulë's people; Gandalf (Olórin) was chosen by Manwë; Radagast (Aiwendil) went at Yavanna's wish; and the two Blue Wizards went into the East. Only Gandalf stayed true to the task to the end.

5Lamps, Trees, Sun and Moon

The Lamps. At first Aulë made two vast lamps, Illuin and Ormal, which Varda filled with light, and Middle-earth bloomed. Melkor threw them down, and their fall broke the land.

The Trees. In Valinor, Yavanna sang two Trees into being: silver Telperion and golden Laurelin. Their light waxed and waned in turn, and their glow was counted in days. Middle-earth lay under starlight all this time.

The Silmarils. Fëanor trapped the blended light of both Trees inside three gems, the Silmarils. After the Trees were killed, only these held their light.

Sun and Moon. From the dying Trees came one last silver flower and one golden fruit. The Valar set them in vessels that sail the sky; Tilion steers the Moon and Arien the Sun. The first rising of the Moon came before the Sun's, and when the Sun first rose, Men woke.

6The waking of the Elves

Varda made the brightest stars, and in that moment the Elves woke beside the mere of Cuiviénen in the East. The first thing they saw was starlight, and they always loved Varda most of the Valar.

Melkor found them first and his creatures stole some away. When Oromë found them, the Valar went to war to protect them and broke Melkor's fortress. Then they invited the Elves to Valinor. Those who said yes, the Eldar, made the Great Journey West in three companies; on the way some stopped and some turned aside. That is the split shown on Plate III.

Elves do not age or die of sickness. When one is killed, or fades from sorrow, the spirit goes to the Halls of Mandos and may in time return. They are bound to the World for as long as it lasts.

7The waking of Men

Men woke in Hildórien, far in the East, at the first sunrise, which is why they are called the Secondborn, or the Followers. No Vala came to guide them. Morgoth reached them early, and the Edain's own stories hint at a dark choice made in that first time, which they would not talk about.

Some kept walking west, away from the Shadow, until they met the Elves in Beleriand. These were the Edain, the Elf-friends of the three houses.

Men are mortal. When they die their spirits pause in the Halls of Mandos and then leave the World altogether, to somewhere only Eru knows. The Elves called this the Gift of Men, though in time Men came to fear it.

8Aulë and the Dwarves

Aulë could not wait for the Children of Ilúvatar to arrive. In a secret hall under the mountains of Middle-earth he made seven Fathers of the Dwarves, and taught them a language he invented.

Eru spoke to him: Aulë had tried to make something only Eru could make, and his Dwarves could only move when he willed them to. Aulë, sorry, raised his hammer to destroy them, and they shrank back and begged for mercy. Eru had given them true life. But the Elves had to be first, so the Fathers were laid to sleep under the stone until after the Elves woke.

The eldest, Durin, woke alone and founded Khazad-dûm, the great Dwarf city later called Moria. Dwarves say that after death Aulë gathers them in halls of their own in Mandos.

9Yavanna, the Ents and the Eagles

When Yavanna heard about the Dwarves, she worried. They would love stone and metal and cut her trees without a second thought. She went to Manwë, who sat in thought and saw the Music again.

The answer: when the Children of Ilúvatar woke, spirits from far away would come to live among the plants and animals. Some would dwell in trees as shepherds, the Ents, and the Eagles of Manwë would live in the mountains and keep watch. In the Third Age Treebeard told the Hobbits that the Elves had first taught the Ents to speak.

Notes: Jewels, Rings and Kingdoms

10Orcs, Trolls and Dragons

Morgoth could never invent a new people, because real life comes only from Eru. Everything he had came from bending something that already existed.

The best-known account says the first Orcs were bred from Elves captured and tortured in Morgoth's dungeons before the Great Journey. Tolkien was never fully happy with this and tried other ideas, so treat it as the most common story rather than the last word.

Trolls, Treebeard said, were made in mockery of the Ents, as Orcs were of Elves; many of them turned to stone in daylight. Sauron later bred stronger kinds: the Uruks of Mordor, first seen in about Third Age 2475, and the Olog-hai, trolls that could bear the Sun. Saruman bred his own Uruk-hai at Isengard.

Dragons were Morgoth's own breeding. Glaurung was the first fire-drake; Ancalagon the Black was the greatest of the winged dragons. Werewolves and vampires were evil spirits housed in beast shapes.

11The Silmarils and the exile of the Noldor

Fëanor, the greatest craftsman of the Elves, made the three Silmarils and loved them above everything. When Morgoth killed the Trees, killed Fëanor's father Finwë and stole the jewels, Fëanor swore a terrible oath with his seven sons to fight anyone who kept a Silmaril from them.

Most of the Noldor followed him back to Middle-earth against the will of the Valar. On the way they killed Teleri at Alqualondë to take their ships, and the Doom of the Noldor fell on them. The long wars of the First Age followed. In the end one Silmaril went into the sky with Eärendil, one into the sea and one into the fires of the earth.

12Númenor

For their part in the war against Morgoth, the Edain were given an island in the great sea, shaped like a star: Númenor. Elros, Elrond's brother, was its first king. Its people lived roughly three lifetimes of ordinary Men, but were not to sail west beyond sight of their island: the Ban of the Valar.

Over the centuries the Númenóreans grew mighty, proud and afraid of death. Ar-Pharazôn, the last king, sailed to Middle-earth and brought Sauron back as a prisoner. Sauron soon ruled him from the inside, set up a temple to Morgoth and talked him into attacking Valinor itself. The Valar laid down their rule of the World and called on Eru. In Second Age 3319 the sea swallowed Númenor; the World was made round and Valinor taken out of its circles.

Elendil and his sons, the Faithful, escaped in nine ships and set up two realms: Arnor to the north, Gondor to the south.

13The Half-elven and the Choice

Three times an Elf and one of the Edain married: Lúthien and Beren, Idril and Tuor, and Arwen and Aragorn. Their families carry Elvish, Mannish and, through Melian, even Maia blood.

Eärendil and Elwing sailed to Valinor carrying a Silmaril, to beg the Valar for help against Morgoth. Help came, and with it a choice for them and their sons: to be counted among the Elves or among Men. Elrond picked the Elves. His twin, Elros, picked Men, and became Númenor's first king. Elrond's children were offered the same choice, and Arwen gave up her Elvish life to marry Aragorn.

14The Rings of Power

Sauron came disguised to the Elves of Eregion in a fair disguise, calling himself Annatar, "Lord of Gifts", and taught their smiths, led by Celebrimbor, how to make rings of power. In secret, in about Second Age 1600, he made the One Ring in the fires of Mount Doom, a master ring over all the rest. The Elves felt it and took off their rings.

  • The Three (Narya, Nenya, Vilya) were made by Celebrimbor alone and never touched by Sauron. They were kept by Círdan (who gave Narya to Gandalf), Galadriel, and Gil-galad (who gave Vilya to Elrond).
  • The Seven went to Dwarf-kings; some were eaten by dragons, the rest taken back by Sauron.
  • The Nine went to Men, who became the Nazgûl.
  • The One was cut from Sauron's hand by Isildur at the end of the Second Age, slipped into the Anduin, dug up by Déagol, taken by Sméagol, found by Bilbo, and unmade in the fire on 25 March, Third Age 3019.

15Hobbits

Tolkien counts Hobbits as a kind of Men, unrelated to Elves or Dwarves, though nobody knew when they split off. They appear first along the Anduin valley as three peoples: Harfoots, Stoors and Fallohides.

In Third Age 1601 the brothers Marcho and Blanco led a group west over the river Baranduin and founded the Shire. Gollum began as Sméagol, one of a river people much like the Stoors.

16Open questions

Tolkien never meant to explain everything, and some things on this chart are left open on purpose. Tom Bombadil, "Eldest", master of the Old Forest, fits no box at all. We never learn where Ungoliant came from, or the Watcher in the Water, or the nameless things deep below Moria. The Huorns may be Ents grown tree-like or trees grown awake. Whether the Great Eagles are Maiar or living birds, and exactly where Orcs came from, Tolkien wrote about in more than one way. Gil-galad's father also changes between drafts.

Notes: Ages, Origins and Sources

A quick timeline, a one-glance table of how every kind of being came to be, and where this chart comes from.

✦Timeline of the Ages

  • Before the World: Eru makes the Ainur; the Music; the vision; Eä.
  • The Years of the Lamps: the Valar shape the World and light it with two Lamps, until Melkor throws them down.
  • The Years of the Trees: Valinor lit by the Two Trees; the Elves wake under the stars; the Great Journey; the Silmarils made; the Trees killed; the Noldor leave.
  • First Age (about 590 years): the first sunrise and the waking of Men; the wars of Beleriand against Morgoth; Beren and Lúthien; Gondolin falls; the War of Wrath, and Beleriand sinks.
  • Second Age (3441 years): Númenor rises; the Rings are made; Númenor drowns (3319); the Last Alliance of Elves and Men beats Sauron (3441).
  • Third Age (3021 years): the Wizards arrive (about 1000); the Shire is founded (1601); Smaug takes Erebor (2770); Bilbo's adventure (2941); the War of the Ring (3018 to 3019).
  • Fourth Age: the age of Men; the last Ring-bearers sail into the West.

✦Places on the chart

Arda: the Earth, the realm the Valar shaped. Eä: the whole created universe. Aman and Valinor: the Blessed Realm in the far West, home of the Valar. Middle-earth: the lands between, where most of the stories happen. Beleriand: the north-west land of the First Age wars, sunk at its end. Cuiviénen: the lake where the Elves woke. Hildórien: the land where Men woke. Númenor: the island kingdom of the Second Age.

Tolkien's published books: The Hobbit, The Lord of the Rings and its appendices, The Silmarillion, Unfinished Tales and The History of Middle-earth, edited by Christopher Tolkien. Tolkien changed his mind on some details over the years. Where the books disagree, this chart follows The Silmarillion and The Lord of the Rings, and flags the doubt (note 16). If you spot a mistake, tell us in the comments.
